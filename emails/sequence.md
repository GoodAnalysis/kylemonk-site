# Nurture Email Sequence — 6 emails over 12 days

Trigger: visitor submits email on the landing page (via Formspree/Netlify Forms → your email tool, or directly in your ESP).

Tool-agnostic: works in MailerLite, ConvertKit, Brevo, or plain Gmail filters if volume is low.
Every email ends with ONE call-to-action. Emails 1–4 sell the **free audit**; 5–6 sell the **call**.

---

## Email 3 — Day 5

**Subject:** "Days, not months" — how?

> Fair question. Agencies aren't slow because the work is slow. They're slow because of process: discovery decks, status meetings, handoffs between designer → developer → QA.
>
> Here's how a project runs with me:
>
> 1. **15-minute scoping call** — you tell me what it needs to do, I quote in writing. (Day 0)
> 2. **Build sprint** — I design and build the whole thing, you watch it come together live. (Days 1–7ish)
> 3. **Ship** — deployed, domain connected, documented handover, one revision round included. (Launch day)

> One person, no handoffs, no theatre. If a project genuinely needs longer, I say so on the call.
>
> Got a site that needs this treatment? **Reply with the URL** — the 10-point audit is still free and waiting.
>
> — Kyle

## Email 4 — Day 8

**Subject:** What a fast site is actually worth

> Some numbers worth knowing (from Google/SOAST studies — look them up if you like):
>
> - As page load goes from **1s → 3s**, bounce probability jumps **32%**
> - As load goes from **1s → 5s**, it jumps **90%**
> - Nearly **60%** of visits from mobile users abandon pages that take over 3 seconds
>
> Translation: a slow site quietly deletes your customers before they see what you offer. And "slow" includes clunky mobile layouts and confusing navigation, not just load time.
>
> This is the first thing I check in every audit. **Reply with your URL** and I'll tell you where yours stands — free, specific, no obligation.
>
> — Kyle
>
> P.S. If your site scores well, I'll tell you that too. An audit that says "you're fine" is still worth having.

## Email 6 — Day 12 (final)

**Subject:** Last one from me (plus a cheat sheet)

> I'll stop landing in your inbox after this — but before I go, here's the **"is my site costing me customers?" cheat sheet**. Check your own site against it this week:
>
> 1. Does it load in under 3 seconds on your phone, on 4G?
> 2. Can a stranger tell what you do within 5 seconds of landing?
> 3. Is there ONE clear next step (a button, a form, a booking link) above the fold?
> 4. Does it look right on your phone? (Half your visitors are there.)
> 5. Is there a way to capture interest (email capture, lead magnet) from people who aren't ready to buy today?
>
> If you answered "no" or "unsure" to 2+ of those, it's costing you customers. That's fixable faster and cheaper than you think.
>
> The free audit offer stays open — **reply with your URL** anytime. And if you're done with these emails, no hard feelings; reply "stop" and I'll never contact you again.
>
> — Kyle
> Kyle Monk · Web apps, built fast

## Sequence rules

- **Anyone who replies** (with URL or question): pause the sequence, respond personally. A reply is a hot lead — automated emails end.
- **Anyone who books a call**: remove from sequence immediately.
- After Email 6, leave them on the broadcast list (monthly "here's what I shipped" email — best-performing content for freelancers, since every send is a portfolio piece).
